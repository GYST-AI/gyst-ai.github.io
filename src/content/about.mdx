## Why we exist

There are more high school students doing genuine research than there are places to take it. A science fair judges a poster in ten minutes. An academic journal is not built for a first-time author, and most conferences quietly assume a university affiliation behind every submission.

The Global Youth AI & STEM Foundation runs the missing middle: an international conference where student work is submitted as a written paper, reviewed by people qualified to review it, and presented by the student who did it.

## What "peer review" actually means here

Every paper submitted to a GYST-AI conference is evaluated by approximately three independent reviewers on a Technical Program Committee, against the criteria professional venues use — originality and innovation, scientific and technical merit, methodological rigor, significance and impact, relevance to the theme, and quality of writing.

The committee makes acceptance decisions based on those reviews and the overall balance of the program. Not every paper is accepted. That is what makes acceptance mean something.

## How the foundation is organized

GYST-AI is run entirely by volunteers, and it is led by high school students. There is no paid staff. That shapes what we can promise: we answer email as quickly as a volunteer team can, and we announce details only once they are actually settled.

The foundation's officers are listed on the [Leadership](/leadership) page. The Technical Program Committee that reviews papers is separate from the foundation's leadership, and is listed on each conference's own site.

The official working language of our conferences is English. All papers, presentations, and conference communications are conducted in English.

## Who can take part

Students at any recognized high school, anywhere in the world, individually or in a team. There are no geographic restrictions on participation. Schools — including homeschools — need to be recognized by their country's Ministry of Education or equivalent government body.

You do not need a laboratory, a university mentor, or prior publications.

## What we don't do

Being clear about this matters, because plenty of things that look like this are not what they claim to be.

- We do not charge a fee to submit a paper.
- We do not accept papers without review, and we do not guarantee acceptance.
- We are not affiliated with ACM, IEEE, or any other professional body. Our conferences use the publicly available ACM proceedings template for formatting; that is a document template, nothing more.
- We do not sell, share, or resell student data.
- We do not promise indexing, DOIs, or placement in any external database.
