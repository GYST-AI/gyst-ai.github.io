## Why we exist

There are more young students doing genuine research than there are places to take it. A science fair judges a poster in ten minutes. An academic journal is not built for a first-time author, and most conferences quietly assume a university affiliation behind every submission.

The Global Youth AI &amp; STEM Foundation runs the missing middle: an international conference where student work is submitted as a written paper, reviewed by people qualified to review it, and presented by the student who did it.

## What we're organized to do

GYST-AI is dedicated to charitable and educational purposes for young scholars who want to advance their learning and research in the sciences, before they start university.

The fields we cover are biomedical sciences, artificial intelligence, medicine, technology, and related STEM disciplines. Part of the point is access: plenty of students are interested in these fields and have no obvious way in, and closing that gap is as much the work as running the events themselves.

The foundation is organized to host educational conferences, lectures, workshops, research opportunities, mentorship, and competitions for secondary-school students and other learners.

That is what we are set up to do, not a schedule. One of those things is running today: **inCABS 2027**, our inaugural conference. Nothing else has been scheduled. Anything new will appear on this site only once it is real and has actual dates on it. We will not announce an intention and call it a program.

## What "peer review" actually means here

Every paper submitted to a GYST-AI conference is evaluated by approximately three independent reviewers on a Technical Program Committee, against the criteria professional venues use. Those criteria are originality and innovation, scientific and technical merit, methodological rigor, significance and impact, relevance to the theme, and quality of writing.

The committee makes acceptance decisions based on those reviews and the overall balance of the program. Not every paper is accepted. That is what makes acceptance mean something.

## How the foundation is organized

GYST-AI is governed by a Board of Directors, which is currently being finalized. Day to day it is run by volunteer officers, and those officers are high school students. There is no paid staff at all. That shapes what we can promise: we answer email as quickly as a volunteer team can, and we announce details only once they are actually settled.

The board and the officers are listed on the [Leadership](/leadership) page. The board section stays empty there until appointments are confirmed, rather than being filled with names we hope to have. The Technical Program Committee that reviews papers is separate from both, and is listed on each conference's own site.

The official working language of our conferences is English. All papers, presentations, and conference communications are conducted in English.

## Who can take part

The foundation exists for secondary-school students and other learners who have not yet begun postsecondary education, anywhere in the world. There are no geographic restrictions.

Eligibility for a specific program is set by that program. For **inCABS 2027**, submission is open to students at any recognized high school, individually or in a team. Schools, including homeschools, need to be recognized by their country's Ministry of Education or equivalent government body. The full rules are on the conference's own site.

You do not need a laboratory, a university mentor, or prior publications.

## What we don't do

Being clear about this matters, because plenty of things that look like this are not what they claim to be.

- We do not charge a fee to submit a paper.
- We do not accept papers without review, and we do not guarantee acceptance.
- We are not affiliated with ACM, IEEE, or any other professional body. Our conferences use the publicly available ACM proceedings template for formatting; that is a document template, nothing more.
- We are not soliciting donations, and we make no claim about our incorporation or tax status. Our purpose is charitable and educational; that is a statement about what we are for, not about any legal recognition we have obtained.
- We do not sell, share, or resell student data.
- We do not promise indexing, DOIs, or placement in any external database.
